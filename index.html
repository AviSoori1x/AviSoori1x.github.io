§01Current work

Robotics foundation models, and how to know when they work

At Mistral I work on vision-language-action models: the policies themselves, the System-2 planners that decompose a long-horizon instruction into something a policy can execute, and the training data behind both.

A lot of my current attention goes to evaluation. Robot policies are easy to demo and hard to measure, so I build real-to-sim pipelines that turn a single real camera frame into a physically-valid simulated scene — a reproducible, gradeable environment instead of an anecdote.

Robotics is the current problem rather than the whole arc. At Mistral I have also been a lead contributor on Shieldstral, a multimodal safety classifier, and on Mistral Small Creative. My deep learning work started in 2017 — NLP first, then recommender systems and multimodal models — and predates the language model era.

§03Open source
makeMoE ★ 812

A sparse mixture-of-experts language model written from scratch in pure PyTorch — expert modules, top-k noisy gating, and the initialisation and routing details that actually decide whether an MoE trains. Built in the spirit of Karpathy's makemore, as a readable reference for the architecture behind Mixtral, DBRX and Grok.

Used as teaching material by the NeurIPS 2024 tutorial “Dynamic Sparsity in Machine Learning”, whose MoE notebook states it is “inspired by the makeMoE blog post” and trains on data pulled straight from the repo; and listed as assigned reading by the M2L Summer School 2024.

seemore ★ 260

A vision language model implemented from scratch in pure PyTorch: a ViT image encoder, a multimodal projection module, and a character-level autoregressive decoder, assembled end to end so every connection is visible. A follow-up, seeMoE, swaps the decoder for a mixture-of-experts version.

Its architecture diagram is used as the reference VLM diagram in Lab 1 of École Polytechnique's graduate course CSC_52002, Multimodal Generative AI.
